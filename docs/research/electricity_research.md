## kaggle

### Takeaways

- Kaggle coverage limit. I could not open any Kaggle notebook for this dataset. Kaggle pages render only through JavaScript (WebFetch returned only the page title), and the Kaggle API needs credentials that this machine does not have. Web searches found no Kaggle notebook clearly built on LD2011_2014. Every number above comes from GitHub repos and files I opened directly with gh/WebFetch.
- Your deep model losing to seasonal naive and ridge matches other projects on this dataset. It is not by itself evidence of a bug. In mstarckgh/ele680 (10 clients, L=168, multi-client input, z-scored), a plain GRU gets test MAE 16.49 kW vs naive_168h 14.43 and naive_24h 14.46. Plain LSTM gets 17.87 and TCN 16.57. In FerAHMz/lab9-dl, all LSTM RMSEs (about 189.6-190.1) are above SeasonalNaive168 (182.45).
- Residual learning on top of seasonal naive is the fix that worked on this exact dataset. In ele680, predicting a correction to last week's load moves the GRU from MAE 16.49 to 14.49 (RMSE-noDST 29.65 to 27.88) and the TCN to 13.97 / 26.53, which beats both naive baselines.
- When deep models do win at short horizons, the margin over ridge or linear models is tiny. timfak557 (MT_001, t+1) has LSTM RMSE 2.1433 vs ridge 2.1481 at a 168-h window, and 2.1673 vs 2.1992 at 24 h. In both day-ahead labs, DLinear/NLinear/MLP beat every LSTM variant. Your ridge at 79.1 vs seasonal naive at 94.1 is a strong but plausible result, and a GRU at 103-108 is below that bar.
- A big difference between your setup and every reference. None of them forecasts one client from a 168-h window of 155 other clients. Single-client work (timfak557, BTL_HOCSAU, zanifer, CosmicKittu) uses only the target's own history, plus calendar features in some cases. TFT and DeepAR-style work uses per-series history with series id. ele680 uses 10 clients in and out. A 155x168 input with roughly 24k training windows is a likely cause of your train-50 / val-130+ kW gap. This is a design issue, not a coding bug. Worth checking in code: is MT_235's own past actually inside the input tensor (not dropped as 'the target'), and is it scaled with the same statistics as the label?
- Preprocessing conventions in the references. (1) Hourly mean of the 4 quarter-hour readings, used by all. (2) Pre-start zeros are treated as missing and cut: TFT replaces 0 with NaN and keeps the active range, timfak557 and CosmicKittu drop leading zeros, and ele680, zanifer, Machoudi and emily-ramond start at 2012-01-01. BTL_HOCSAU keeps only clients that are complete from 2011 (158 of 370). (3) Scaling is per series and fit on train only (TFT per-id StandardScaler, ele680 per-client z-score, others MinMax on train). (4) DST rows are mostly kept unchanged (zanifer, BTL_HOCSAU, timfak557). FerAHMz adds a DST fix step, and sreelekhanampally found that the duplicated DST hour caused a fake +87% jump in the aggregate. (5) Windows are 168 h in most deep setups (TFT encoder, ele680, zanifer, the labs). BTL_HOCSAU found its LSTM did not benefit from 168 h vs 24 h (MAE 29.090 vs 28.248).
- Clients with a strong daily cycle are hard to beat. In the PatchTST repo, seasonal naive 24h is the best model on 63 of 370 customers and seasonal naive 168h on 55, and fine-tuning beats SN24 by only about 4.1% MAE pooled. BTL_HOCSAU reports that only 4 of 122 clean clients have lag-24 autocorrelation below 0.90. MT_235 (SN R2 0.838) fits this regime, where learning the residual over seasonal naive or ridge is the natural design.
- Distribution shift between val and test is common with chronological splits on this data. BTL_HOCSAU reports MT_162 levels of train 293.5 / val 255.1 / test 285.0 kW and warns that the val period is a different regime. Your val seasonal naive (117.8) being much worse than test (94.1) points the same way. Early stopping on such a val set at epoch 6-10 is expected. It is not a sign of label misalignment.
- Cautionary example. The widely copied Keras-book LSTM on LD2011_2014 (Packt Ch. 6) fits MinMaxScaler on the whole series and builds the target as data[i + NUM_TIMESTEPS + 1]. It also reports no baseline. Do not use tutorials like this as evidence that LSTMs should easily win here.

## papers

### Takeaways

- Your result is plausible and is not clear proof of a bug. In LSTNet Table 2 (Electricity, h=3) a plain RNN-GRU gets RSE 0.1102. A univariate linear AR gets 0.0995. So the GRU loses to linear by about 11% at h=3 and by about 23% at h=24 (0.1295 vs 0.1054).
- Deep models beat linear on this data by only a small margin. LSTNet-Skip beats AR by about 13% at h=3 (0.0864 vs 0.0995) and by about 4.5% at h=24 (0.1007 vs 0.1054). PatchTST beats DLinear by about 8% MSE at T=96 (0.129 vs 0.140). DLinear beats every Transformer on Electricity by 17-27%.
- Using many clients as input hurts. LSTNet reports multivariate LRidge (RSE 0.1467 at h=3) worse than univariate AR (0.0995). They blame overfitting from richer input. PatchTST reports channel-mixing at 0.205 MSE vs channel-independent at 0.136 (T=96). It says channel-mixing models overfit after a few epochs. This matches your 155-channel GRU, whose best epoch is 6-10 and whose train/val gap is large.
- The published fix for losing to linear is a linear skip. LSTNet adds a linear AR forecast to the network output (Y = h_D + h_L). Removing it gave the largest ablation drop. Learning a residual over ridge or over seasonal naive is the same idea.
- Most published deep wins on this dataset come from global models. DeepAR, TFT, N-BEATS and the Monash deep baselines train one model across 321-370 series (500k windows for DeepAR and TFT). Each predicts a series from its own history and calendar. None of them predicts one client from 155 other clients. Your setup (one target, about 24k windows, 155 exogenous channels) is the regime where the literature finds linear models win.
- Normalization in the literature varies. LSTNet code divides each series by its max. The LTSF benchmark (DLinear, PatchTST baselines) uses a per-channel StandardScaler fit on train, which is global like yours. TFT z-scores each entity separately. DeepAR divides by 1 + window mean. NLinear subtracts the last value. RevIN and PatchTST z-score each input window and undo it on the output. On Electricity, DLinear says there is no obvious shift, and NLinear is no better than Linear. PatchTST instance norm gains only about 3% (0.129 vs 0.133). Normalization is unlikely to close a 79 vs 103-108 RMSE gap alone.
- Daily seasonality dominates this data. TFT finds hour-of-day is the most important input, ahead of past usage. It finds local recurrent processing can hurt on Electricity, while attention to previous days helps. This supports giving the model explicit lag-24 and lag-168 inputs, or a seasonal-naive baseline to correct.
- Published numbers cannot be compared directly with your per-client R2. LSTNet RSE pools all 321 series and divides by variance around the global mean, so cross-client variance inflates the denominator. LTSF MSE is in z-scored units over all channels at horizons of 96 or more. TFT, DeepAR and N-BEATS report ND or quantile loss on a 1-week test window. No source reports a single-client seasonal-naive RMSE on UCI.
- I found no credible Kaggle notebook with single-client short-horizon numbers on UCI ElectricityLoadDiagrams20112014. One search turned up nothing usable. Alonso et al. 2019 (arXiv 1910.06640) reports an LSTM about 24% better than seasonal naive at 24 h ahead. But it uses London smart-meter data, not UCI.
- Exact RevIN ECL numbers were not retrieved because OpenReview blocked automated access. Only the method and the setup are confirmed. Paper PDFs and extracted text are in /tmp/claude-1000/-home-vungocduong-dl-final-prj/3c29501b-74ce-4dfe-b0a4-0fed46b25312/scratchpad/papers.

- **Lai et al. 2018, Modeling Long- and Short-Term Temporal Patterns with Deep Neural Networks (LSTNet), SIGIR 2018** (https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.07015)

## pitfalls

### Takeaways

- The sources point to a method problem more than a code bug. The signature is train error about 50 kW, val RMSE 130 to 146 kW against a val seasonal-naive of 117.8, best epoch 6 to 10, and ridge on 6 lags winning. That is the documented pattern of a high-capacity, channel-dependent model overfitting under year-to-year drift (Han et al. 2023, PatchTST Table 7, DLinear). Leakage bugs (scaler fitted on all data, shuffling across splits) make models look better, not worse (arXiv 2512.06932, up to 20.5% RMSE gain from leakage). They cannot explain the deep model losing.
- The biggest likely fix is cutting the 155 client channels. On the same UCI Electricity data, channel mixing vs channel independence for the same backbone is MSE 0.196 vs 0.136 (PatchTST Table 7, horizon 96). Transformer CD vs CI is 0.250 vs 0.185 (Han et al. Table 4). The study closest to this project (Portuguese load, 2302.12168) used target-only inputs. Adding temperature there changed LSTM MAPE only from 2.23 to 2.20 and made N-BEATS worse (1.90 to 2.00). Recommend a target-only GRU (target history plus calendar), optionally with a few highly correlated clients, as the main ablation.
- Make the network's job a residual. Options are (a) predict y[t+h] - y[t+h-24] or y[t+h] - y[t+h-168], so the zero output equals seasonal naive (the mlforecast tutorial uses Differences([24,168])), (b) subtract the last observed value as in NLinear and PRReg, or (c) add a linear skip path such as a ridge or AR head and let the RNN learn the correction. This guarantees the model starts at baseline quality instead of having to rediscover the daily cycle.
- Feed the seasonal lags explicitly. The ridge wins because y[t+h-24], y[t+h-48] and y[t+h-168] are aligned to the target hour. The Kaggle Web Traffic 1st-place seq2seq GRU fed year, half-year and quarter-ago values to the encoder and decoder, because GRU/LSTM 'gradually forget' old items. Pass the same target-aligned lags into the output head or decoder at each horizon step. This re-checks the off-by-h alignment bug already found in the ridge.
- Add RevIN or instance normalization (normalize each window by its own mean and std, denormalize the output) for inter-year level shift. PatchTST reports Electricity MSE 0.129 with vs 0.131 without it, a small gain. The gain was large on Traffic (0.367 vs 0.425). It is cheap and model-agnostic (github.com/ts-kim/RevIN).
- Training protocol matters a lot. The tuned Portuguese-load LSTM used look-back 240, hidden size 48 (much smaller than 2x128 bi-GRU), 180 epochs and a 30-seed median ensemble. A single LSTM had RMSE 259.1 +- 16.4 vs 213.19 for the ensemble. The Web Traffic winner avoided early stopping on a noisy val set and averaged 30 checkpoints. Stopping at epoch 6 to 10 on a drifting val set is noisy. Seed or checkpoint averaging and a smaller hidden size are standard remedies.
- A bidirectional encoder is not a leak if the window contains only past data. It adds capacity, though, so a unidirectional, smaller GRU is a reasonable ablation. Look-back of 168 is fine. PatchTST on Electricity improves only from 0.138 to 0.130 going from 192 to 336.
- Implementation checks still worth running. (1) model.eval() and torch.no_grad() at val/test, and do not compare train-mode loss (dropout on) with eval RMSE. (2) Task 2 decoder must run with teacher forcing 0 at val and test, including the early-stopping metric (exposure bias, Teutsch and Mader 2022). (3) Scaler fitted only on train, and the target inverse transform uses the train mean and std. (4) No hidden state carried across batches or splits. (5) The target index equals t+h for every horizon. (6) Seasonal naive and lag features use the same t+h-24 alignment as the label. (7) A sanity test where the model gets y[t+h-24] as input and zero weights elsewhere should reproduce RMSE 94.1.
- Context for the report. Losing to seasonal naive or ridge without these fixes is common and well documented (Makridakis et al. 2018. fpp3 benchmark rule. DLinear beats all Transformers on Electricity, 0.140 vs 0.193 MSE. Elsayed et al. GBRT on lag windows beats LSTNet, DARNN and DeepGLO on subsampled Electricity, RMSE 119.05 vs DeepGLO 141.29). It is normal to report these baselines honestly and show which fixes close the gap.
- Data caveat. In UCI, clients created after 2011 are stored as zeros. Standard benchmarks (LSTNet, Informer, DLinear) use 321 clients over 2012-2014. Informer's univariate setup uses only MT_320 as the target. This also supports moving to target-only or few-channel inputs to reduce shift.
